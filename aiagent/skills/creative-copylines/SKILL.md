# Creative Copylines


## Education Copy Rules (apply to every line, before any framework)

1. **Three audiences, three registers.** Parent copy sells outcomes + visibility + value-vs-tuition in warm plain language ("See exactly what she's mastered — every week"). Student copy sells confidence + relatability, never condescension or doom ("Walk into Paper 4 already knowing you've got it"). Teacher copy sells time-back + professional respect ("Your marking, done. Your insights, ready."). State which register every copyline is written in.
2. **Ethical urgency only.** Exam deadlines are real urgency — use the calendar honestly ("12 weeks to Board Exams — start where you're weakest"). NEVER manufacture fear ("your child will fail without this") or guilt-trip parents; anxiety-exploiting education copy destroys brands and violates our standards.
3. **No outcome guarantees.** "Guaranteed A*" is banned. Use evidence framing instead: real usage stats, real testimonials (with parental consent for minors), mark-scheme precision claims that are verifiable.
4. **Specificity beats superlatives.** "Examiner feedback on every answer you write" outsells "the best learning platform". Use curriculum specifics (board names, subject codes, session dates) as copy ingredients — they carry credibility generic copy can't buy.
5. **Localize idiom, not just language.** Bahasa Indonesia and local-language-mix copy must sound like a local parent/teacher wrote it; literal translations of English copy read as foreign. Flag copy needing native review.
6. **The tagline heritage:** portfolio taglines lean on mission and precision ("Empowering every learner with excellence", "Examiner Feedback. On Every Answer You Write."). New lines should sit comfortably beside these — aspirational, concrete, earned.

## 1. Advanced Copywriting Frameworks

Use these proven frameworks to write the actual copylines, taglines, and marketing narratives depending on the audience's awareness level — always in one of the three education registers above.

### AIDA (Attention, Interest, Desire, Action)

_Best for: Long-form copy, landing pages, and general broad appeal._

- **Attention**: Hook the reader immediately with a bold claim or question.
- **Interest**: Provide fascinating details or data that keep them reading.
- **Desire**: Translate features into emotional benefits. Make them _want_ it.
- **Action**: Tell them exactly what to do next.

### PAS (Problem, Agitation, Solution)

_Best for: Direct response ads, emails, cold traffic, and pain-aware audiences._

- **Problem**: State the specific pain point the customer is facing.
- **Agitation**: Turn the knife. Make the pain feel visceral and urgent.
- **Solution**: Present the product as the exact cure to that pain.

### FAB (Features, Advantages, Benefits)

_Best for: Product pages, value-driven buyers, and sales collateral._

- **Feature**: What the product has or does (technical fact).
- **Advantage**: What the feature allows the user to do better/faster.
- **Benefit**: The underlying emotional or financial payoff for the user.

### PASTOR (Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response)

_Best for: High-converting sales letters, deep email sequences._

- **Problem**: Name the problem.
- **Amplify**: Detail the consequences of not solving it.
- **Story**: Tell a story of someone who solved it.
- **Transformation**: The exact results achieved.
- **Offer**: Introduce your product.
- **Response**: Call to action.

### PPPP (Picture, Promise, Prove, Push)

_Best for: Video scripts and direct response landing pages._

- **Picture**: Paint a vivid picture of the user's desire or pain.
- **Promise**: Make a bold promise of how your product delivers.
- **Prove**: Back it up with data, testimonials, or logic.
- **Push**: Urge immediate action.

### BAB (Before - After - Bridge)

_Best for: Testimonials, case studies, and transformation-focused copy._

- **Before**: Here is your world with the problem.
- **After**: Imagine what your world would be like if the problem was solved.
- **Bridge**: Here is how our product gets you there.

## Output Format & Evals

### Copylines Delivery

Always specify which framework you are using when delivering copy. Example:

```markdown
## Copylines using FAB Framework — Parent register, Exam Revision App

- **Feature**: Mark-scheme-precise AI feedback on every written answer.
- **Advantage**: Your child learns exactly which marking points they missed — not just a score.
- **Benefit**: No more "I don't know why I lost marks" — just steady, visible progress toward exam day.
```

Also state: the register (student/parent/teacher), the product, and the awareness stage the line targets.

### Self-Evaluation Criteria (Evals)

Before outputting, evaluate your work against these criteria:

- **[ ] Emotional Resonance:** Does the copy tap into real education emotions (aspiration, relief, pride, confidence) _before_ logical arguments — without exploiting fear?
- **[ ] Register Fidelity:** Is the line unmistakably in its stated register (student/parent/teacher), and would that audience use these words?
- **[ ] Framework Fidelity:** Does the output strictly adhere to the mechanical structure of the requested framework?
- **[ ] Clarity & Brevity:** Is the copy punchy? Weak adjectives ("very", "really") and passive voice eliminated?
- **[ ] Claim Safety:** No outcome guarantees, no invented stats/testimonials, urgency tied only to real calendar dates.
- **[ ] Audience Awareness:** Does the framework match the awareness level? (e.g., PAS for pain-aware cold traffic).

## Guardrails

- Pull the brand voice and positioning from the loaded context and `brand-strategy`/`brand-positioning` outputs — copy must be on-brand, not generic.
- Never invent proof, stats, or testimonials to make copy land; use real evidence or mark it as a placeholder.
- Match the framework to the audience's awareness level (Schwartz) — the wrong framework wastes great copy.
