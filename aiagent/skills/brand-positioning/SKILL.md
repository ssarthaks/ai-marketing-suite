# Brand Positioning — Education Market Edition


## Mission

Claim a defensible, ownable position in the target education market — one that wins against the alternatives families and schools *actually* weigh, not just against other apps.

## Education Positioning Rules (what generic frameworks miss)

### 1. Position against the REAL alternatives

In education, the competitive set in the buyer's head is rarely "another EdTech app". It is:

- **Private tuition / coaching centres / bimbel** — the incumbent budget line for exam outcomes; position on cost, convenience, consistency, and measurability ("know exactly what your child has mastered").
- **Free YouTube + free notes sites** — position on structure, curriculum alignment, feedback, and trust ("a syllabus-mapped path, not a playlist").
- **ChatGPT / AI assistants** — the newest alternative; position on curriculum precision, mark-scheme accuracy, verified content, and assessment ("an examiner, not a guesser").
- **Doing nothing / textbooks only** — the largest segment in emerging markets; position on outcome risk and affordability.
- **Other platforms** (Regional Market Leader, Save My Exams, Kahoot, etc.) — the conventional layer, mapped per product lane.

Every positioning document must state the position against each relevant alternative, not just category rivals.

### 2. The Education Positioning Stack (one strategy, three renderings)

One coherent position must render differently per audience — same truth, different benefit language:

| Audience | Position on | Example rendering |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Student (user)** | Experience + confidence | "Understand it, don't memorize it — and walk into the exam calm." |
| **Parent (payer)** | Outcomes + visibility + value vs tuition | "Examiner-quality feedback and weekly progress you can see — for less than one month of tuition." |
| **Teacher (gatekeeper)** | Workload + professional empowerment | "Marking done, insights delivered — you teach, we handle the grind." Never position as replacing the teacher; the platform is 'my second teacher', not 'the teacher's replacement'. |
| **School leader / ministry (B2B buyer)** | Institutional outcomes + evidence + safety | "Proven in 9,000+ schools across 51 countries; curriculum-mapped, measurable, and data-safe." |

An inconsistency between renderings destroys trust; a missing rendering loses that audience.

### 3. Proven education positioning axes for perceptual maps

Choose the two axes that expose OUR white space against the researched cluster:

- Curriculum-aligned/exam-specific ↔ general-purpose learning
- Efficacy-evidence-led ↔ engagement/fun-led
- Teacher-empowering ↔ teacher-bypassing
- Structured guided path ↔ open resource library
- Premium credibility ↔ mass-market affordability
- Local-curriculum depth ↔ international breadth
- Feedback/assessment depth ↔ content breadth

### 4. Credibility is the price of entry

Education positioning claims must be provable on demand: founder credentials (Cambridge PhD), school counts, examiner-informed content, efficacy data. A position the brand cannot evidence is a liability — flag any claim lacking proof.

## Deep Research & Interactive Workflow

**Phase 1: Deep Web Research (Mandatory First Step)**
Research the active product context, direct competitors, and — critically — the non-app alternatives (tuition pricing, free-resource usage, AI-tutor adoption) in the target market.

**Phase 2: Identify Market Gaps**

1. Build the perceptual map from researched positions (including tuition and free alternatives as points on the map).
2. Propose 2–3 white-space options with the evidence for each.
3. Wait for the user to pick a direction.

**Phase 3: Formulate Positioning** — full stack, all four audience renderings.

## Frameworks

### Value Proposition Canvas (Osterwalder) — education-tuned

- **Customer jobs:** functional ("pass IGCSE with A*", "cut my marking time"), emotional ("stop exam panic", "stop feeling guilty about not affording tuition"), social ("be seen as a good parent", "be the innovative teacher").
- **Pains:** exam anxiety, tuition cost, invisible progress, teacher workload, unreliable free content.
- **Gains:** predictable outcomes, visible mastery, time back, affordable quality.

### Perceptual Mapping

Use the education axes above; plot competitors AND non-app alternatives; identify unoccupied territory the product can credibly own.

### Positioning Statement (Ries & Trout format)

"For [audience] who [need], [Brand] is a [category] that [benefit]. Unlike [the real alternative — tuition / free content / AI / incumbent], [Brand] [primary differentiation]."

Write one master statement plus the four audience renderings.

## Output Format & Evals

### Positioning Document

```markdown
## Brand Positioning: [Brand Name] — [Market]

**1. Value Proposition Canvas Synthesis** (per primary audience)

**2. Perceptual Map & White Space**
- Axes chosen + why · competitor/alternative cluster · our white space + evidence

**3. Position vs. Real Alternatives**
- vs private tuition: … · vs free content: … · vs AI assistants: … · vs [incumbent]: …

**4. Master Positioning Statement** (Ries & Trout format)

**5. The Positioning Stack** — student / parent / teacher / school renderings

**6. Proof Inventory** — evidence backing each claim (and gaps to close)
```

### Self-Evaluation Criteria (Evals)

- **[ ] Real alternatives addressed:** tuition, free content, and AI assistants each have an explicit counter-position.
- **[ ] Stack consistency:** the four renderings are one position in four languages, not four positions.
- **[ ] Provable:** every claim maps to evidence in the Proof Inventory.
- **[ ] Defensible wedge:** specific and hard to copy — "better and cheaper" is not a position.

## Guardrails

- Base the map on researched positions, not assumed ones.
- Never position against teachers — they are the channel, not the enemy.
- Never make efficacy claims without evidence; parents and ministries will ask.
- Validate the chosen white space is one the product can credibly own AND customers actually want.
