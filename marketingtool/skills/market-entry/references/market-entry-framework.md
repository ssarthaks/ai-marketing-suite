# Market Entry Frameworks

Decision frameworks for entering a new geography or segment. Keep recommendations tied to researched evidence and the loaded `.agents/<productname>/product.md` context. Use placeholders (`[Target Market]`, `[Our Product]`) rather than hard-coding a product.

---

## 1. Ansoff Matrix — confirm the growth vector

|                     | **Existing Product**           | **New Product**            |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| **Existing Market** | Market Penetration (not entry) | Product Development        |
| **New Market**      | **Market Development** ← usual | Diversification (riskiest) |

Most EdTech/SaaS expansion is **Market Development**: same product, new geography. Product risk is low; the real risk is in **localization and distribution**, so weight the plan there.

---

## 2. CAGE Distance Framework — how "far" is the market?

| Dimension          | Ask                                                 | Why it matters                  |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Cultural**       | Language, education culture, religion, social norms | Drives localization + messaging |
| **Administrative** | Regulation, currency, trade ties, licensing         | Drives legal cost + speed       |
| **Geographic**     | Distance, time zones, infrastructure, logistics     | Drives ops + support model      |
| **Economic**       | Income, price sensitivity, payment infrastructure   | Drives pricing + unit economics |

Score each Low/Med/High. High distance on any axis = budget and timeline must expand for that axis.

## 3. Entry Mode Decision Tree

```
Is there a strong local distributor / school network you can partner with?
├─ Yes → Is the buyer institutional (B2B/schools)?
│        ├─ Yes → Channel / Reseller or Local Partnership (fast, low risk, med control)
│        └─ No  → Local Partnership for distribution + your own brand on top
└─ No  → Is the product digital-first with a strong brand signal?
         ├─ Yes → Organic / Direct entry (slow, high control, low–med risk)
         └─ No  → Is speed critical and capital available?
                  ├─ Yes → Acquisition or Joint Venture (fast, higher risk)
                  └─ No  → Licensing (low risk, low control) or delay entry
```

| Mode               | Risk    | Speed   | Control | Best when                          |
| ------------------ | ------- | ------- | ------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Organic (direct)   | Low–Med | Slow    | High    | Digital-first, strong brand        |
| Local Partnership  | Low     | Fast    | Med     | Distributor/school network exists  |
| Channel / Reseller | Low     | Med     | Med     | B2B; institutions are the buyer    |
| Joint Venture      | Med     | Med     | Low     | Regulation requires local entity   |
| Acquisition        | High    | Fastest | High    | Need speed + an existing user base |
| Licensing          | Low     | Fast    | Low     | Monetize IP without operations     |

## 4. Market Attractiveness Scorecard (1–5 each)

| Dimension                | Weight | Notes                                           |
| ------------------------ | ------ | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Market size              | High   | Bottom-up reachable paying base                 |
| Growth rate              | High   | Category CAGR + local adoption curve            |
| Competitive intensity    | Med    | Porter's Five Forces summary                    |
| Regulatory friendliness  | Med    | Licensing, data residency, foreign-entity rules |
| Cultural fit             | Med    | CAGE cultural distance                          |
| Infrastructure readiness | Med    | Device/data penetration, payment rails          |

Overall < 3/5 → reconsider or stage entry as a small pilot first.

---

## 5. Localization Checklist (non-negotiable for entry)

- [ ] Language & translation (UI, content, support)
- [ ] Curriculum / domain alignment (for EdTech: local exam boards + syllabus)
- [ ] Pricing & currency (anchor to local willingness-to-pay, not home prices)
- [ ] Payment methods (local wallets/gateways, cash-on-delivery if relevant)
- [ ] Customer support (hours, language, channel — WhatsApp/Viber/Line as needed)
- [ ] Legal & compliance (entity, tax, data-protection law)
- [ ] Brand & creative (locally resonant imagery, tone, social proof)

---

## 6. Phased Roadmap Logic

- **Phase 1 — Foundation (Month 1–2):** legal/localization/pilots. Metric: pilots signed, beta users.
- **Phase 2 — Traction (Month 3–4):** small paid tests, first revenue, feedback loop. Metric: active users / MRR.
- **Phase 3 — Scale (Month 5–6):** double down on the winning channel, activate referral. Metric: scaled users / MRR.

Never scale spend before Phase 2 proves a channel works locally — home-market channel performance rarely transfers unchanged.
