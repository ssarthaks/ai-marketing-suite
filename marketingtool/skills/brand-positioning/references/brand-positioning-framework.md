# Brand Positioning Research Framework

## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

This framework defines how a brand competes and provides value in the marketplace. It focuses on the external market perspective—identifying unmet customer needs and mapping the competitive landscape to find "white space." Use this document to guide the AI Agent in structuring competitive positioning and formulating compelling value propositions.

## 2. Research Methodology

Before applying the frameworks below, the Agent must execute the following research steps:

1. **Map the Competitive Landscape**: Identify 3-5 direct competitors and 2-3 indirect competitors or alternative solutions.
2. **Customer Pain Point Mining**: Review competitor negative reviews and customer support forums to identify the most severe unmet needs.
3. **Attribute Selection**: Identify the top two purchase-driving attributes for the category (e.g., Price, Speed, Complexity, Customization).

## 3. Core Frameworks & Analysis

### 3.1 The Value Proposition Canvas (Osterwalder)

A tool to ensure the product offering is perfectly aligned with what the customer actually needs.

**Part A: The Customer Profile**
| Element | Definition | Research Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)** | Functional, social, or emotional tasks the customer is trying to complete. | Move beyond features; what outcome are they buying? |
| **Pains** | Negative outcomes, risks, and obstacles related to the job. | What frustrates them about current market solutions? |
| **Gains** | Required, expected, or desired benefits. | What does success look like in their own words? |

**Part B: The Value Map**
| Element | Definition | Alignment Check |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Products/Services** | The specific offerings provided. | Does this directly address the JTBD? |
| **Pain Relievers** | How the product explicitly eliminates customer pains. | Does this map 1:1 with the highest-friction pain point? |
| **Gain Creators** | How the product produces the expected/desired outcomes. | Does this exceed standard market expectations? |

### 3.2 Perceptual Mapping (Market Landscape)

A visual technique used to analyze a brand's position relative to competitors based on key attributes.

| Axis            | Recommended Variables                              | Agent Task                                                  |
| :-------------- | :------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **X-Axis**      | Price, Complexity, Target Size (SMB vs Enterprise) | Determine the primary constraint of the buyer.              |
| **Y-Axis**      | Quality, Speed, Customization, Feature Depth       | Determine the primary desired outcome of the buyer.         |
| **White Space** | The unoccupied quadrant.                           | Identify the quadrant with high demand but low competition. |

## 4. Synthesis & Actionable Insights

Translate the research into the definitive Positioning Statement using the Ries & Trout formula.

### The Positioning Statement Formula

> "For **[Target Audience]** who **[Statement of Need/JTBD]**, **[Brand Name]** is a **[Product Category]** that **[Key Benefit/Gain Creator]**. Unlike **[Primary Alternative/Competitor]**, our product **[Primary Differentiation/Pain Reliever]**."

**Actionable Translation:** Once the statement is defined, use it to generate the H1 Headline and Subheadline for the product's homepage.

## 5. Guardrails & Common Mistakes

- **Feature-Centric Pains**: Pains should be emotional or business-impact driven (e.g., "losing hours on weekends"), not feature-driven (e.g., "no export button").
- **Vague Differentiation**: The differentiation must be a specific, provable claim, not subjective fluff like "we care more."
- **Crowded Quadrants**: If the Perceptual Map places the brand in the exact same quadrant as the market leader, force a re-evaluation of the X and Y axes to find a defensible niche.
