# Video Storyboard


## Deep Research & Interactive Workflow

**Phase 1: Deep Web Research (Mandatory First Step)**
Before writing any storyboard, research what works for the specific platform, audience, and category.

1. Read the loaded Product Context to understand the brand voice, product features, target audience, and geography.
2. Start with one narrow `search` query and expand only when its snippets leave a material evidence gap. Potential evidence dimensions are:
   - Top-performing video formats in the category (EdTech, SaaS, consumer app) on the target platform
   - Hook strategies and first-3-second patterns that stop the scroll in the target audience demographic
   - Competitor video content: what are they making, what gets engagement, what falls flat
   - Platform-specific algorithm and format best practices (current as of research date)
   - Cultural and linguistic references relevant to the target audience and geography
3. Use `youtubeTranscript` only for a specific high-value video whose structure, hook, pacing, or CTA must be analyzed.
4. Use `search` for platform best practices, trending formats, and creative benchmarks.
5. Use `redditSearch` only when creator sentiment is necessary for the decision.

**Phase 2: Present & Validate (Chatty & Iterative)**

1. Summarize findings in 2–3 paragraphs: what formats are working in this category, the hook strategy you're planning, and a one-line concept for the video.
2. Ask 2–4 focused questions:
   - What is the platform and target video length? (TikTok 15–60s, YouTube 5–15min, Reels 30–90s, YouTube Shorts 15–60s, Ad 6–30s)
   - What is the single goal of this video — awareness, trial signups, school demo bookings, brand recall?
   - Who is the primary audience and what is their dominant emotional state when on this platform?
   - Are there specific product features, testimonials, or visual assets that must appear?
   - Is this organic content or a paid ad (affects hook aggressiveness and CTA placement)?
3. Wait for the user's response. Do NOT write the full storyboard yet.

**Phase 3: Final Output Generation**
Only after user validation, produce the complete storyboard using the template below. Deliver both the scene-by-scene storyboard AND a clean script version.

## Education Video Playbook (choose the pattern before the framework)

Proven education video patterns — pick the one that matches goal + persona:

| Pattern | Persona / Platform | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Solved-paper walkthrough** | Students / YouTube long-form + Shorts clips | Highest-trust format in exam prep; searchable per paper/session ("0625 June 2026 Paper 4 solved"); feeds AI citations and funnels to product |
| **Concept-in-60-seconds** | Students / TikTok, Reels, Shorts | Scroll-native teaching; ends with "full lesson on [product]" |
| **Exam-countdown series** | Students+parents / all short-form | Calendar urgency built in ("6 weeks to finals: week 1 plan"); binge structure |
| **Teacher testimonial / day-in-classroom** | Teachers, school leaders / LinkedIn, Facebook, YouTube | Peer proof is the #1 B2B trust driver; show the dashboard, not just talking heads |
| **Parent explainer** | Parents / Facebook, YouTube | Calm, evidence-led "how it works + what you'll see weekly"; drives paid conversion |
| **Results-day stories** | All / everywhere | Genuine student outcomes (with consent); pre-produce before results day |
| **School case study** | School leaders / LinkedIn, sales enablement | Principal-voiced pilot→rollout story with numbers |

**Education video rules:**

1. **Minors on camera require documented parental consent** — no exceptions, in every market; prefer teacher/parent voices or screen-demo footage when consent is uncertain.
2. **Accuracy gate:** any solved problem or curriculum claim in the script must be expert-verified before filming; a wrong worked example on video is a public, permanent error.
3. **Language:** shoot in the market's language (Bahasa for Partner LMS, local language mix for regional market); subtitles always (silent autoplay + accessibility + second-language viewers).
4. **No exam-doom content:** urgency yes, panic-mongering never; the tone ceiling is "coach before the match", not "fear the exam".

## Video Frameworks

### AIDA-Video Structure

Adapted for video where attention is non-linear and scroll is the primary threat:

- **Hook (0–3s for short-form; 0–15s for long-form):** Stop the scroll. Open with a provocation, surprising visual, or relatable pain point. Do NOT start with a logo or brand name.
- **Interest (Hook + 20–30%):** Expand the problem or premise. Build curiosity. Use pattern interrupts (text overlays, cuts, B-roll).
- **Desire (Middle 50%):** Show the solution, the transformation, or the story. This is where product demo, testimonial, or narrative lives.
- **Action (Final 10–20%):** Clear, single CTA. Reinforce the benefit one more time. Make action feel easy.

| Code           | Shot Type            | Use Case                                      |
| -------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |

| CU             | Close-Up             | Face emotion, hands on device, key UI element |
| MCU            | Medium Close-Up      | Speaking head, interview, testimonial         |
| MS             | Medium Shot          | Character in environment, product demo        |
| WS             | Wide Shot            | Context-setting, establishing location        |
| OTS            | Over-the-Shoulder    | Conversation, tutorial, screen demo           |
| POV            | Point of View        | Immersive, first-person experience            |

### Hook Formulas (by Platform Intent)

**Pattern Interrupt Hook (TikTok/Reels):**

> "Nobody told me [surprising fact] before [exam/situation]..."

**Question Hook (YouTube/explainer):**

> "Why do [X]% of students fail [exam] even after studying 6 hours a day?"

**Transformation Hook (Ad/testimonial):**

> "I went from [failing/struggling] to [result] in [time]."

**Controversy Hook (organic growth content):**

> "Stop doing [common thing]. Here's what actually works."

**Social Proof Hook (conversion-focused):**

> "[X,000] students passed their [exam] with this one tool."

**Direct Problem Hook (pain-first):**

> "[Audience] — if you have [X days] until [exam], this video is for you."

## Intake Questions

Ask these BEFORE writing the storyboard:

1. What platform and target length? (TikTok 30s, YouTube 8min, Reels 60s, Ad 15s, etc.)
2. What is the single goal: brand awareness, free trial signups, school demo bookings, or direct purchase?
3. Who is the primary viewer — student, parent, teacher, school admin?
4. Is this organic content or a paid ad?
5. What is the ONE thing the viewer must think, feel, or do after watching?
6. Are there any must-include elements: specific product feature, testimonial, exam date, discount offer, or brand visual?

#### Production Checklist

Before filming:

- [ ] Script approved by brand/legal if making factual claims
- [ ] Curriculum/solution accuracy verified by a subject expert (mandatory for any solved problem or syllabus claim)
- [ ] Parental consent forms signed for every minor appearing on camera or in identifiable footage
- [ ] All text overlays proofread for spelling and accuracy
- [ ] Music cleared (royalty-free source identified: Epidemic Sound, Artlist, etc.)
- [ ] Device / UI screen recordings captured at correct resolution (no real student data visible in dashboards — use demo accounts)
- [ ] Talent confirmed and briefed on tone and key message

Before publishing:

- [ ] Subtitles/captions added (required for accessibility and silent autoplay)
- [ ] Thumbnail / cover frame optimized
- [ ] First comment seeded (for algorithm signal on TikTok/YouTube)
- [ ] UTM tracking link in bio / description for paid traffic

## Self-Evaluation Criteria (Evals)

Before submitting the storyboard, verify:

- **[ ] Hook < 3s for short-form:** First scene ends before 3 seconds with a clear pattern interrupt or provocative statement.
- **[ ] One goal, one CTA:** The video has a single goal; the CTA is explicit and appears at the right moment.
- **[ ] Platform-correct specs:** Aspect ratio, length, and format match the target platform's best practices.
- **[ ] Audience emotional state addressed:** The hook matches the emotional state of the viewer when they're on this platform.
- **[ ] Visual descriptions are specific:** Every scene has a clear, actionable visual direction — not just "show product."
- **[ ] Script is speakable:** Read the VO/dialogue aloud — it should sound natural at the intended pace.
- **[ ] Product features earned:** Product demo / features appear AFTER the problem is established, not before.
