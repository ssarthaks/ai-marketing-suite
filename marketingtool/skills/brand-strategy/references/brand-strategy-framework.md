# Brand Strategy Research Framework

## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

This framework defines the "Why" and "Who" of a brand, moving beyond visual aesthetics to establish the core DNA. It provides the structured methodologies required to define Brand Core (Identity), Market Positioning, and Execution pathways. Use this document to guide the AI Agent in structuring deep brand research and translating it into an actionable strategy.

## 2. Research Methodology

Before applying the frameworks below, the Agent must execute the following research steps:

1. **Analyze the Founder/Company Voice**: Review the primary website, about pages, and CEO/Founder communications to extract authentic mission and values.
2. **Audit Competitor Identities**: Review 3-5 direct competitors to identify the dominant brand archetypes in the category.
3. **Identify Target Persona Desires**: Analyze customer reviews (G2, Trustpilot, Reddit) to understand what the target audience _aspires_ to be (Reflection/Self-image).

## 3. Core Frameworks & Analysis

### 3.1 Kapferer's Brand Identity Prism

A tool to map the internal and external facets of a brand's identity to ensure 360-degree consistency.

| Facet            | Definition                                     | Research Question for Agent                                                          |
| :--------------- | :--------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Physique**     | Tangible, physical, or visual traits.          | What visual cues (colors, UX, style) represent this category?                        |
| **Personality**  | The brand's character and tone of voice.       | If this brand were a person speaking, what adjectives describe them?                 |
| **Culture**      | Core values and organizational principles.     | What fundamental belief drives this company?                                         |
| **Relationship** | The nature of customer interaction.            | Is the brand a mentor, a peer, a servant, or a rebel?                                |
| **Reflection**   | The aspirational image of the target customer. | Who does the customer _want_ to be seen as when using this?                          |
| **Self-Image**   | How the customer feels internally.             | What private, internal emotion does the user feel when succeeding with this product? |

### 3.2 Jungian Brand Archetypes

Used to anchor the brand's personality in universally understood psychological patterns.

| Archetype                | Core Desire    | Strategy                 | Example Brand     |
| :----------------------- | :------------- | :----------------------- | :---------------- |
| **The Creator**          | Innovation     | Develop artistic skills  | Apple             |
| **The Caregiver**        | Protect others | Help others              | Johnson & Johnson |
| **The Ruler**            | Control        | Create order             | Rolex             |
| **The Jester**           | Enjoy life     | Be playful               | Old Spice         |
| **The Regular Guy/Girl** | Belonging      | Be down to earth         | IKEA              |
| **The Lover**            | Intimacy       | Become attractive        | Victoria's Secret |
| **The Hero**             | Mastery        | Be strong and competent  | Nike              |
| **The Outlaw**           | Liberation     | Disrupt the status quo   | Harley-Davidson   |
| **The Magician**         | Power          | Transform situations     | Disney            |
| **The Innocent**         | Safety         | Do things right          | Dove              |
| **The Explorer**         | Freedom        | Experience new things    | The North Face    |
| **The Sage**             | Understanding  | Seek truth and knowledge | Google            |

### 3.3 Aaker's Brand Equity Model

A holistic system to define the brand vision across four perspectives.

| Perspective               | Focus Areas                                | Actionable Insight                                             |
| :------------------------ | :----------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Brand as Product**      | Scope, attributes, quality, use cases.     | Define the functional superiority.                             |
| **Brand as Organization** | Innovation, culture, local/global focus.   | Highlight corporate social responsibility or innovation speed. |
| **Brand as Person**       | Brand personality, customer relationships. | Define the specific tone of voice guidelines.                  |
| **Brand as Symbol**       | Visual imagery, heritage, metaphors.       | Identify the core metaphors used in marketing copy.            |

## 4. Synthesis & Actionable Insights

Once the frameworks are populated:

1. **Define the Golden Circle**: Extract the _Why_ (Purpose), _How_ (Culture/Process), and _What_ (Product).
2. **Select the Primary Archetype**: Choose _one_ primary archetype to dictate the copywriting tone.
3. **Draft Messaging Pillars**: Create 3-4 core messaging pillars based on Aaker's "Brand as Product" and "Brand as Organization".

## 5. Guardrails & Common Mistakes

- **Avoid Generic Values**: Do not accept or generate values like "integrity" or "quality" unless qualified with a specific, unique angle.
- **Do Not Hallucinate DNA**: Base the Culture and Personality heavily on the provided product context or actual website copy.
- **Ensure Differentiation**: If the competitor analysis shows the entire market uses the "Hero" archetype, explicitly propose an alternative (e.g., "The Explorer" or "The Sage") to create differentiation.
