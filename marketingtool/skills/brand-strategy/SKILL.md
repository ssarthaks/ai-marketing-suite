# Brand Strategy — Education Market Edition


## Mission

Define a brand DNA that earns the two hardest currencies in education — **family trust** and **institutional credibility** — while staying genuinely loved by students who have zero tolerance for corporate cringe.

## Education Brand Strategy Rules

### 1. Trust is the category entry fee

Education brands are trusted with children and their futures. Every brand decision filters through: "Would a careful parent trust this? Would a principal defend choosing it?" Playful is allowed (Interactive Quiz App proves it); careless, hype-y, or manipulative never is. Efficacy claims, credentials, and institutional proof are brand assets — build them into the identity, not just the marketing.

### 2. Education-proven archetypes (choose ONE primary)

| Archetype | Fits when | Portfolio example | Risk to manage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Sage** (wisdom, mastery, truth) | Credibility-led, exam-outcome brands | Exam Revision App (examiner-precision), EduPlatform's pedagogy-first DNA | Can feel cold — pair with warmth |
| **The Caregiver** (support, reassurance) | Parent-facing, anxiety-reducing brands | "Second teacher" promise itself | Can feel soft on rigor |
| **The Hero** (achievement, courage) | Aspiration, national-mission narratives | EduPlatform Regional — "Empowering every learner with excellence" | Can pressure already-anxious students |
| **The Jester/Explorer** (fun, discovery) | Engagement-led, teacher/classroom brands | Interactive Quiz App, K-12 LMS Platform ("explore knowledge") | Must still signal learning outcomes to adults |

The strongest education brands run **one primary archetype + a Sage undertone** — fun brands still need credibility; serious brands still need humanity.

### 3. The Three-Audience Voice Matrix (mandatory output)

One brand, three registers — define all three or the brand fractures in execution:

| Dimension | Student voice | Parent voice | Teacher/School voice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Register | Peer-like, encouraging, zero condescension, meme-literate but never try-hard | Warm, credible, reassuring, plain-language | Professional, respectful of expertise, evidence-based |
| Promise language | "You've got this — here's exactly how" | "See real progress, every week" | "Better outcomes, less workload, full visibility" |
| Never | Corporate jargon, guilt, exam-doom | Fear-mongering, tech-babble, overpromising | Implying teachers are replaceable or the problem |

### 4. National-mission narratives (emerging markets)

In developing and emerging markets, education brands can credibly carry national-aspiration narratives ("transform the nation through education"). These are powerful with ministries, press, and parents — but must be earned with visible commitment (local team, local curriculum, local pricing), or they read as marketing colonialism.

### 5. Portfolio brand architecture

We run a **house of brands** (EduPlatform, K-12 LMS Platform, Interactive Quiz App, Online Learning Academy, Exam Revision App) with Advanced Pedagogy as the endorsing institutional parent for B2B credibility. When strategizing any single brand, state: what it borrows from the parent (credibility, pedagogy, scale proof) and what it must own alone (local identity, audience-specific personality). Free properties (Exam Prep Platform) are funnel brands — light identity, heavy utility, visibly connected to their monetizing sibling.

## Deep Research & Interactive Workflow

**Phase 1: Deep Web Research (Mandatory First Step)**
Research the active product's existing brand expression, competitor brand strategies in its lane (how do Regional Market Leader, Save My Exams, Kahoot present?), and cultural context for the market.

**Phase 2: Present & Validate (Chatty & Iterative)**

1. Summarize current/potential brand strategy briefly.
2. Ask 2–4 focused questions to validate archetype, DNA, and tone direction.
3. Wait for the user's response.

**Phase 3: Final Output Generation** — the full Brand Strategy document.

## Frameworks

### Kapferer's Brand Identity Prism (education-tuned)

- **Physique:** visual identity per brand guidelines (colors, mascots — e.g., quiz-master personas; UI as brand surface for a product used daily).
- **Personality:** the archetype voice.
- **Culture:** pedagogy-first values, founder credibility, "education transforms lives".
- **Relationship:** the "second teacher" relationship — supportive authority, not vending machine.
- **Reflection:** the aspirational user ("a student who works smart", "a parent who invests wisely", "an innovative teacher").
- **Self-image:** how users feel — capable, supported, ahead.

### Aaker's Brand Identity & Equity Model

Brand as Product (curriculum coverage, feedback quality) · as Organization (global scale + local commitment) · as Person (archetype) · as Symbol (visual assets, taglines).

### Keller's CBBE Pyramid

Salience → Performance & Imagery (exam results + classroom joy) → Judgments & Feelings (credible + warm) → **Resonance** (the goal: teachers evangelize, parents refer, students defend the brand in group chats).

## Output Format & Evals

### Brand Strategy Document

```markdown
## Brand Strategy: [Brand Name] — [Market]

**1. Brand Core (Golden Circle)** — Why / How / What

**2. Brand Identity Prism (Kapferer)** — all six facets, education-specific

**3. Brand Archetype** — primary + Sage-undertone note; voice adjectives

**4. Three-Audience Voice Matrix** — student / parent / teacher registers with do/don't examples

**5. Trust & Proof Assets** — credentials, scale proof, efficacy evidence woven into identity

**6. Portfolio Architecture Note** — what this brand borrows from the parent vs. owns alone

**7. Messaging Pillars** — 3–4 pillars, each with a one-line proof point
```

### Self-Evaluation Criteria (Evals)

- **[ ] Alignment:** the Why connects directly to the What; no purpose-washing.
- **[ ] Distinctiveness:** archetype + voice clearly distinguishable from the researched competitor set.
- **[ ] Three voices defined:** a copywriter could write student, parent, and teacher copy from this document without follow-up questions.
- **[ ] Trust-bearing:** the identity carries proof, not just personality.

## Guardrails

- Ground in the product's existing brand guidelines and live research — not generic brand theory.
- Commit to ONE primary archetype; a brand that tries to be everything is forgettable.
- Never build a voice that mocks, pressures, or guilt-trips students; never a parent voice built on fear.
- Distinguish strategy (internal Why) from positioning (external claim) — hand positioning to `brand-positioning`.
