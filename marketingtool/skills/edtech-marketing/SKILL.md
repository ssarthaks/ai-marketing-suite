# EdTech Marketing Strategy


## Deep Research & Interactive Workflow

**Phase 1: Deep Web Research (Mandatory First Step)**
Before answering any EdTech marketing questions, conduct deep research. Do NOT rely on training knowledge alone.

1. Read the loaded Product Context to anchor strategy in the specific product, curriculum alignment, pricing model, and geography.
2. Start with one narrow `search` query and expand only when its snippets leave a material evidence gap. Potential evidence dimensions are:
   - EdTech market trends in the target geography (growth rates, device/internet penetration, digital learning adoption)
   - Competitor EdTech products: their pricing, user acquisition channels, school partnership models
   - Academic calendar and exam cycle for the target region (key exam dates, school terms, enrollment windows)
   - Parent and student behavior: Reddit communities, Facebook groups, YouTube channels for study tips
   - Teacher influencer communities and professional development networks (Facebook groups, WhatsApp groups, LinkedIn)
   - Government EdTech policy: subsidies, approved vendor lists, curriculum integration requirements
   - Successful EdTech marketing campaigns in comparable markets (India, Indonesia, Philippines)
3. Use `search` for market data, competitor discovery, and trend signals.
4. Use `redditSearch` only when community sentiment is necessary.
5. Use `youtubeTranscript` only when a specific video's structure or messaging is necessary.
6. Use `extract` for precise fields on competitor pricing or partnership pages; reserve `reader` for whole-page arguments.

**Phase 2: Present & Validate (Chatty & Iterative)**

1. Synthesize research into a 2–3 paragraph brief covering: market maturity, key acquisition channels used by competitors, academic calendar hotspots, and initial strategic hypothesis.
2. Ask 2–4 focused questions:
   - Is the primary go-to-market B2C (direct to students/parents) or B2B2C (through schools)?
   - Which exam or curriculum is the product aligned to, and when are the major exam dates?
   - What is the current biggest acquisition challenge: awareness, conversion, or retention?
   - What budget and channels are currently active?
3. Wait for the user's response. Do NOT generate the full strategy yet.

**Phase 3: Final Output Generation**
Only after user validation, compile the full EdTech Marketing Strategy using the template below.

## EdTech-Specific Marketing Frameworks

### Multi-Stakeholder Decision Model

EdTech purchasing involves 3–5 distinct stakeholders, each with different motivations:

| Stakeholder        | Core Motivation                    | Key Fear                       | Decision Power       | Best Channel to Reach               |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Student**        | Pass exams / reduce anxiety        | Failure, peer judgment         | High (self-directed) | TikTok, YouTube, Instagram          |
| **Parent**         | Child's academic success           | Wasted money, screen time      | High (pays)          | Facebook, WhatsApp, SMS             |
| **Teacher**        | Make lessons effective             | Extra workload, bad tools      | Med (influencer)     | Facebook groups, LinkedIn, Email    |
| **School Admin**   | Institutional outcomes, compliance | Budget accountability          | High (institutional) | B2B sales, BD outreach, conferences |
| **Government/NGO** | Systemic education improvement     | Vendor lock-in, accountability | Variable             | Tenders, MOU, policy papers         |

Each channel, message, and campaign must be designed for a specific stakeholder, not a vague "user."

### Academic Calendar Marketing Moments

EdTech demand is extremely seasonal. Map all campaigns to the academic calendar:

| Period                            | What's Happening                | Marketing Opportunity             | Message                          |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Back to School (start of term)    | New school year anxiety         | Subscription sign-up drive        | "Start strong this term"         |
| Mid-Term Exam Prep                | First internal exams            | Free trial + conversion           | "Ace your mid-terms"             |
| Year-End / Board Exam Prep (peak) | High-stakes exams               | Paid campaign peak spend          | "One platform, every past paper" |
| Post-Result / Re-enrollment       | Results out, next year planning | Upsell to next grade              | "Level up for next year"         |
| Summer / Holiday                  | Low school pressure             | Long-form content, brand-building | "Learn ahead, stress less"       |
| Parent-Teacher Meetings           | Parents are school-engaged      | B2B2C school integration pitch    | "What your school uses"          |

### EdTech Acquisition Funnel (AARRR variant)

**Awareness →** Student/Parent discovers product via TikTok/YouTube/Google/School
**Activation →** Free trial, free chapter, or free past paper download
**Retention →** Daily/weekly active usage tied to exam schedule + parent progress reports
**Referral →** Student ambassador programs, "invite a friend" mechanics, teacher referrals
**Revenue →** Subscription conversion, school license, bundle upsell

### B2C vs B2B2C Strategy Matrix

| Element      | B2C (Direct to Student/Parent) | B2B2C (Through Schools)                    |
| ------------ | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| CAC          | Higher (paid ads, content)     | Lower (school champions product)           |
| LTV          | Lower (individual sub)         | Higher (school license = multi-year)       |
| Sales Cycle  | Hours–days                     | Weeks–months                               |
| Trust Signal | Exam pass rates, testimonials  | School endorsement, teacher recommendation |
| Best Channel | Social ads, SEO, YouTube       | BD team, school visits, edu conferences    |
| When to Use  | Consumer-first, freemium       | Institutional, curriculum-aligned product  |

### The School Sales Pipeline (B2B motion in detail)

The proven school-acquisition sequence — run it as a pipeline with stage conversion metrics:

1. **Target list** — segment schools by type (private English-medium first: budget + autonomy), size, curriculum, and fee band; map the local decision web (owner/board vs principal vs academic coordinator).
2. **Warm entry** — teacher champions from freemium usage, referrals from existing pilot schools, distributor/OEM introductions (the Acer for Education pattern), education-conference contacts. Cold outreach is the last resort; peer-reference entry converts multiples better.
3. **Demo → pilot design** — pilot 1–3 classes for one term, free or nominal cost, with success criteria agreed upfront (usage %, assessment improvement, teacher satisfaction). A pilot without agreed success criteria is a free trial that never closes.
4. **Enablement during pilot** — teacher onboarding/training in local language, WhatsApp/Viber support group, monthly usage reviews with the coordinator. Usage in weeks 1–4 predicts the deal.
5. **Proof report → close** — end-of-term report (usage data + outcomes + teacher quotes) presented to the principal/board; propose whole-school rollout timed to the next academic year's budget cycle.
6. **Rollout → renewal engine** — year-round seat-utilization monitoring (the renewal predictor), termly impact reports to the principal, parent-facing showcase (the school markets US to parents), case-study co-marketing (see `co-marketing`).
7. **Reference flywheel** — every successful school becomes a visit site and case study for its district/network; principals trust neighboring principals more than any ad.

**Ministry/government motion (where relevant):** long cycles (6–18+ months), driven by policy alignment (digital-education programs), local presence, and evidence; deliverables are MOUs, approved-vendor listings, and co-funded pilots. Never build a revenue plan on ministry deals alone; treat them as credibility multipliers for the school pipeline.

**Distributor/OEM playbook (the K-12 LMS Platform pattern):** device bundling (Acer), publisher partnerships (textbook publishers own school relationships — the Intan Pariwara model), telco/payment partnerships for B2C distribution. Structure: partner brings the relationship, we bring the platform and enablement; co-branded materials and shared pipeline reviews.

## Intake Questions

Ask these BEFORE generating the strategy:

1. Is the primary go-to-market B2C (students/parents buy directly) or B2B2C (schools adopt and recommend)?
2. Which exam boards, curricula, or grade levels does the product serve? (e.g., IGCSE, A-Level, SEA, Higher Secondary Board)
3. What are the 3 biggest exam dates / academic milestones in the target region and when are they?
4. What is the current average CAC and what channels are already active?
5. What is the biggest drop-off point in the current funnel — acquisition, trial activation, or paid conversion?
6. Is there a freemium tier? What is the free-to-paid conversion rate?

#### 5. Content Strategy by Stakeholder

**For Students:**

- TikTok: "5 past paper tricks for [Exam] — in 60 seconds" series
- YouTube: Subject-specific revision tutorials tied to exam syllabus
- Instagram: Visual memory aids, infographic study guides

**For Parents:**

- Facebook: "How [Product] helped [student] pass their [Exam]" case studies
- WhatsApp broadcast: Monthly progress tip + upcoming exam calendar
- Google Search: "[Exam] preparation guide for parents" blog content

**For Teachers:**

- LinkedIn: Thought leadership on pedagogy + digital learning integration
- Facebook Groups: Active participation in teacher communities (no hard sell)
- Email newsletter: Classroom resource packs, teaching materials

**For School Admins:**

- Case studies: School-level outcome data (pass rates, engagement)
- ROI calculator: "What 1 license costs vs. private tuition"
- Conference presence: Education sector events, Ministry-hosted summits

#### 7. Referral & Virality Mechanics

- **School ambassador:** Top student in each school gets branded ambassador kit + free subscription

## Self-Evaluation Criteria (Evals)

Before submitting the strategy, verify:

- **[ ] Stakeholder-specific:** Each major touchpoint is designed for a specific stakeholder, not a vague "user."
- **[ ] Academic calendar anchored:** Campaign timing maps to the actual exam calendar for the target region.
- **[ ] B2C/B2B2C clarity:** The strategy is explicit about which mode to prioritize and when to use each.
- **[ ] Conversion path defined:** There is a clear free→paid or trial→subscription conversion flow with specific triggers.
- **[ ] Metrics are specific:** Every channel has a measurable KPI, not just "grow awareness."
- **[ ] 90-day plan is executable:** Month 1 tasks can be actioned in the next 2 weeks.
- **[ ] No generic advice:** Strategy references the specific product, curriculum, and region — not a template.
